The agency sells residence and citizenship programs in several countries. A typical buyer asks a lot of questions before a first call: requirements, timelines, costs, documents.

Leads arrive through many channels at once — website forms on two sites, paid social, TikTok, WhatsApp and other messengers. Managers were answering the same questions over and over, and leads that wrote at night waited until morning.

{% endblock %} {% block built %}
  • Main AI agent on the CRM’s open lines: answers from a knowledge base of programs and country presentations, qualifies, offers a consultation.
  • Two-line setup for TikTok leads in a separate CRM: a first-line agent replies fast and filters, a second-line agent goes deeper with qualified leads.
  • Lead routing to managers, including temporary reassignment when someone is on leave.
  • Form protection on both websites: phone numbers that cannot exist are rejected at the form, before they become fake leads.
  • n8n automations between websites, CRM and messengers, with failure alerts.
{% endblock %} {% block extra %}

What this project taught me

Silent failures are the expensive ones

A step that times out but reports success looks fine for weeks. Every production flow now has alerts on the real outcome, not on “request sent”.

Agents need a change log

Every edit to an agent’s instructions or knowledge base is recorded, so any odd answer can be traced to a specific change.

One fix, both websites

When a business runs two sites, every change is applied to both and checked on both. Half-applied fixes create the strangest bugs.
